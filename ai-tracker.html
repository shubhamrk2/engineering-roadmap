The six levels

Choose the level you can demonstrate today, not the one you expect to reach.

LevelNameWhat it means in practice
0Not startedNo exposure yet.
1Read / watchedYou have read or watched it. You could not yet explain it unprompted.
2UnderstandYou can explain it in your own words and predict what code will do.
3With guidanceYou can implement it with hints, documentation, or an assistant explaining.
4IndependentYou implemented it from a blank file with no assistant and no search, and it works. Evidence required.
5Debug + trade-offsYou can find root causes in unfamiliar code, change it for a new requirement, and argue why this design over another. Evidence required.
Red pencil — what counts as evidence

A repository or commit link, a passing test run you can reproduce, a dated gate result, or a recording of you explaining it. “I did the course” and “I understand it” are not evidence. If an assistant wrote the code, the level is 3 at most, no matter how well it runs.

Weekly review

Five questions, every Saturday. Copy as text produces a summary with your skill levels that you can paste into the chat for a proper review.

What I will do with your weekly review

I will tell you what I think is real and what is not, name the single most valuable thing to fix next week, and say whether you are on schedule. If the evidence does not support a level you set, I will say so plainly.
